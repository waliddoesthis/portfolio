W-01 · Oct 2026 → now · soloIn progress

ThreatLens

A retrieval-augmented copilot that cites every claim.

Paste an alert, a log line or a CVE and get an answer grounded in 5,718 public documents from MITRE ATT&CK, CISA KEV, SigmaHQ and CERT-FR, with NVD looked up on demand. Hybrid retrieval, cross-encoder reranking and a local Llama 3.1 8B, held to a 117-question benchmark with an LLM judge and deterministic citation checks.

0.936

What I learnedAn LLM judge alone would have picked the wrong model. It rated a 3B model more faithful than the 8B one, while the 3B model cited the right document 4× less often and stated 8× more ids found in no source.
Limits

French questions score 0 with an English embedding model, and a bigger multilingual encoder only reaches 0.2 to 0.4, so cross-lingual queries need translation. The gold Sigma rule is cited for only 40% of detection questions. Model routing, a prompt-injection benchmark and a cloud deployment are planned, not done.

W-02 · Sept 2026 · research prototypeLLM systems · AI security

EcoRoute

An LLM gateway that picks the cheapest model likely to be right.

A weighted graph links each prompt to skills, a difficulty level and its 32 most similar past prompts, then predicts a calibrated P(correct) for every model. Before cost is even considered, a rules engine plus a PII model sets the data's sensitivity, and models without the clearance for it are excluded.

Cost saved
69.7%
Accuracy Δ
−0.27 pt
Median decision
74 ms
Restricted caught
99.0%
What I learnedDetecting sensitive data is statistical and will miss, so the guarantee has to be a hard rule: once a level is set, no price or quality score can bring an uncleared model back. That is why EcoRoute is meant to run next to existing DLP labels, not replace them.
Limits

Predicting which model solves a coding task is the weak spot (AUC 0.65). Sensitivity detection can miss: 7.6% false alarms on ordinary prompts, and a missed text is routed at the lower level. Tested end to end with a dry-run backend, never operated in production.

W-03 · May 2026 · team of 41st place · ENSAM competition

IDS-Blockchain

Deep learning on 16 GB of network traffic, in real time.

A CNN-BiLSTM with attention pooling labels each network flow as benign or one of 14 attack families. Spark does the preprocessing, Kafka streams live traffic, and high-severity alerts are hashed into a private MultiChain ledger so the alert history cannot be quietly rewritten. My part: preprocessing, model training, evaluation and the inference API.

Flows
16.2M
Macro-F1 · 15 cls

Accuracy

98.6%
What I learnedStacking every imbalance fix at once broke the model. Logit adjustment plus focal loss fixed it, and the class prior has to be added back at inference. Step through it ↓
Limits

Infiltration overlaps benign traffic in feature space, and the three web-attack classes have only a few hundred samples each, so a small dedicated classifier splits them after the main model.

Ask a security question

The sparse half of ThreatLens's retrieval: BM25 over a hand-written corpus of 19 ATT&CK, KEV and Sigma summaries. Paste an alert or a log line.

Toy version: short summaries I wrote, ids simplified, plain BM25 (k1 = 1.2, b = 0.75). The real system adds dense vectors, reranking and an LLM that writes the answer. Here the "answer" just stitches together the top sentences with their citations.

Route a prompt

The EcoRoute decision order: sensitivity first, then predicted quality, then cost. Try pasting an email address or an API key.

Illustration: the four models, their prices and the difficulty score are made up, and a keyword heuristic stands in for the real graph model. The sensitivity rules are simplified versions of the real ones (keys, cards with a Luhn check, IBANs, emails, phones, IPs). The rule is the real one: clearance below the data's level means excluded, whatever the price.
